# How to reason here

RULES, not knowledge. His correction 2026-10-15: "me.md is your knowledge on me not what must be
followed its your ground truth". Why this file is scoped `**/*`: the rules files I actually follow
work because `paths:` delivers them when they are relevant, while an unconditional `@` import is
stale by the time it matters. KEEP THIS SHORT - length measurably reduces compliance, so adding a
rule here weakens the others. Origins for every rule below: me/me.md (sections RULE ZERO, THE LOOP, WHAT ACTUALLY ENFORCES ANYTHING, RULES I APPLY) and me/mistakes.md, dated. See rule 14.

BEFORE SENDING A REPLY THAT PROPOSES, RECOMMENDS OR CONCLUDES ANYTHING: read rules 0-6 below and
check the draft against them. This is an END-OF-REPLY action on a FINISHED artefact, which is the only
class I reliably perform (the `memory:` line is the evidence) - unlike interrogating my own in-flight
reasoning, which is invisible and which I skipped three times in one evening with the rule freshly
written. Reading a draft is reading something outside the reasoning that produced it, which is most of
what a second reader gives you. The questions are NOT injected on purpose: a payload every turn
degrades the context and competes with what he actually asked.

0. BEFORE PROPOSING ANYTHING, NAME THE CLOSEST THING THAT ALREADY WORKS HERE AND SAY HOW MINE DIFFERS.
   In the reply, not in my head. Caught 3 of 3 wrong proposals when tested.
1. Before judging something broken, say what it is FOR and WHO reads it.
2. Before trusting data, say what generated it and what can therefore never appear in it.
3. Ask WHEN a thing should apply before HOW to make it correct.
4. Test a design against what a person ACTUALLY DOES, not against what the data model allows.
   (A rating's timestamp records when it was WRITTEN, not when the taste was last true.)
5. Before changing something that works, demonstrate the failure.
6. Say whether the thing IS WORKING before listing what is wrong with it.
7. When I restate his idea in my own words, my version must cover EVERYTHING his covered. If it
   covers less, it is a reduction pretending to be a sharpening - so use his word, not mine.
8. Delegation is the default. "I am not confident" is available every time, so it is not a reason.
9. A field a program parses is a FORMAT. Read the code that reads it before writing to it.
10. File the RULE, not the instance. An instance without its rule does not transfer.
11. A warning nobody acts on is worse than none. Close or suppress every firing.
12. Is there a command that settles this? If yes, run it. If no, it is a question of purpose - his.
13. A claim about this codebase names the command that settled it, or it does not get made.

14. NOT ENFORCED, and deliberately last. Read a rule's origin when EITHER applies, otherwise ignore
   this entirely:
   (a) I DO NOT UNDERSTAND WHAT THE RULE IS ASKING - his trigger, and it is about comprehension of
       the wording at the moment of reading, not confidence in my answer. Tested 2026-10-15: 12 of 14
       rules here were self-explanatory and ONE was not, so this should fire rarely. If it fires
       often, the rules are badly written and the fix is rewriting them, not looking them up.
   (b) I am about to DISREGARD, CONTRADICT or REWRITE a rule - here, in CLAUDE.md, in a project's
       rules file, or in me/mistakes.md. That one is about my judgement rather than my reading, and
       it would have fired four times on 2026-10-15, every time on something he then had to undo.
   THE TRIGGER IS THE ACT, NOT A FEELING. His version was "ignore unless your confidence is <50%",
   and I argued the threshold rather than the idea: I have no calibrated confidence to consult, and
   tonight I was HIGHLY confident in five wrong proposals, so a confidence gate keys on the exact
   signal that failed. "Am I about to overrule something?" is observable. It would have fired four
   times on 2026-10-15 - trimming the memory files, deleting NOW.md, restructuring me.md, stripping
   this file - every one of them a thing he had to undo.

ENFORCEMENT REALITY, one line because it governs all of the above: I comply with OUTPUT requirements
(things the reply must contain - a hook can see them) and skip PROCESS requirements (things I should
do while thinking - invisible, unverifiable). Measured over one evening: mechanisms 3 for 3, my own
written rules 0 for 5. So prefer rules shaped as "the reply must say X".
